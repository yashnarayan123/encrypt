WHAT THIS MEANS (teacher requirements 3 and 4):
  (3) Performance: pure-Python AES manages ~100 KB/s where the
  C/OpenSSL reference does millions of KB/s -- absolute numbers
  suit a laptop demo only; a real drone uses hardware AES. ECB/CBC
  decrypt costs ~3x encrypt (inverse MixColumns does twice the
  field multiplications); GCM decrypt costs the same as encrypt
  (CTR uses forward blocks only). GCM costs about the same as CBC
  here (CTR + GHASH are cheap next to AES rounds).
  (4) AES-256 costs ~1.4x AES-128, exactly as the round count says;
  its brute-force margin (2^256 vs 2^128, both infeasible) is
  theoretical. It does NOT fix mode flaws: Task 3 broke ECB-256
  identically (splices obeyed, silhouette visible), and Task 4's
  GCM stops every attack at BOTH key sizes. Mode beats key length.
